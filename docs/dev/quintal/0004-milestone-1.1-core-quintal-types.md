## Concept Cards

### Pitch and Pitch Class (The Complete Musician)

**Quick Definition:** A pitch is a specific musical tone at a particular frequency and register; a pitch class groups together all pitches sharing the same letter name across all octaves.

**Core Definition:** Pitch refers to the relative highness or lowness of sound, generated by a vibrating body. The speed of regularly recurring vibrations (the frequency) determines the pitch. Pitch class refers to all pitches with the same letter name regardless of octave. There are 12 pitch classes in the chromatic system. Pitches at a distance of one or more octaves share the same pitch class and letter name.

---

### Pitch Class (Introduction to Post-Tonal Theory)

**Quick Definition:** A pitch class is a group of pitches related by octave and enharmonic equivalence, all sharing the same (or enharmonically equivalent) letter name.

**Core Definition:** A pitch class is a collection of pitches related by octave and enharmonic equivalence. There are exactly twelve pitch classes in the equal-tempered system, represented by integers 0 through 11. A pitch class is an abstraction and cannot be adequately notated on musical staves; it is "not a single thing; it is a class of things: namely, pitches one or more octaves apart." Pitch classes reside in circular pitch-class space (mod 12), not linear pitch space.

---

### Pitch vs Pitch Class (Open Music Theory)

**Quick Definition:** A pitch is a discrete tone at a specific frequency and octave (e.g., C4), while a pitch class is the group of all pitches related by octave equivalence and enharmonic equivalence (e.g., all Cs, B-sharps, and D-double-flats across all octaves), represented in set theory by integers 0-11 where C=0.

**Core Definition:** In set theory, "class" means "group." Pitch class abstracts away octave and spelling information. The twelve pitch classes are represented with integer notation (0-11, where C=0), visualized on a clock face. This distinction is fundamental to all set-theory operations (Tn, In, etc.).

---

### Set Class (A Geometry of Music)

**Quick Definition:** A set class groups together all chords related by any combination of the five OPTIC symmetries -- including both transposition and inversion -- so that major and minor triads, for instance, belong to the same set class.

**Core Definition:** A set class is an equivalence class formed by all five OPTIC symmetry operations: O, P, T, I, and C. Members share the same sequence of arc lengths on the pitch-class circle, though the sequence may proceed clockwise or counterclockwise. Central to twentieth-century music theory, where composers treated inversionally related chords as equivalent.

---

### Transposition Symmetry (A Geometry of Music)

**Quick Definition:** The T (Transposition) symmetry groups together musical objects that can be transformed into one another by moving all notes in the same direction by the same amount, defining "chord type."

**Core Definition:** Adding T to the OPC symmetries (which define chords) yields OPTC, which defines "chord types" or transpositional set classes. Two chords belong to the same type if one can be rotated into the other in pitch-class space, meaning they share the same sequence of arc-length distances between adjacent notes.

---

### Inversion Symmetry (A Geometry of Music)

**Quick Definition:** The I (Inversion) symmetry groups together musical objects that can be transformed into one another by turning pitch space "upside down," defining the additional equivalence that distinguishes set classes from chord types.

**Core Definition:** Adding I to the OPTC symmetries yields OPTIC, which defines set classes. Inversionally related chords share the same sequence of arc lengths on the pitch-class circle (though potentially in opposite directions). Major and minor triads both divide the circle into arcs of 4, 3, and 5 semitones, just read in opposite directions.

---

### Quartal Harmony (Twentieth-Century Harmony)

**Quick Definition:** A harmonic system in which chords are built by superimposing intervals of the fourth, creating a distinctly twentieth-century sound that stems from both triadic ornamentation and medieval polyphony.

**Core Definition:** Chords by fourths are built by superimposing intervals of the fourth. In other spacings, most of the chord members must be placed a fourth apart to preserve the distinctive quartal sound. This rootless harmony places the burden of key verification upon the most active melodic line. Any member can function as root (like diminished seventh, augmented triad).

---

### Four-Note Quartal Chords (Twentieth-Century Harmony)

**Quick Definition:** Quartal structures of four stacked fourths, more resonant than three-note types due to the consonant tenth between outer voices, extremely useful in their three inverted forms, and capable of resolving smoothly to tertian chords.

**Core Definition:** P4+P4+P4 spans a minor tenth (consonant outer interval). Four-note chords by fourths are extremely useful in their three inverted forms because of the variety of intervals they contain. Moving through inversions of the same chord produces real harmonic movement without root change.

---

### Quartal Voice Leading (Twentieth-Century Harmony)

**Quick Definition:** Principles governing melodic movement in quartal harmony: any chord tone may skip a fourth or seventh if others remain stationary; any three-note quartal chord may progress diatonically, chromatically, or by skip if one voice moves with strong melodic purpose.

**Core Definition:** Quartal voice leading is fundamentally more flexible than tertian voice leading because of the rootless, ambiguous nature of quartal chords. Without traditional root-movement gravity, voices move more freely as long as melodic logic is maintained. The guiding principle is melodic purpose rather than specific interval restrictions.

---

## Mathematical Context

**Z12 (integers mod 12):** Pitch classes are elements of the cyclic group Z12 = {0,1,...,11} under addition mod 12. This models octave equivalence: C=0, C#=1, ..., B=11.

**Pitch-class set:** An unordered subset of Z12. A 4-element pc set is a subset {a,b,c,d} with |{a,b,c,d}| = 4. Two pc sets are the same if they contain the same elements regardless of ordering.

**Interval structure:** For a pc set, the interval structure is the 3-tuple of consecutive intervals in a specific ordering of the 4 pitch classes. The [6,8] constraint requires each component to be in {6, 7, 8}.

**CRITICAL: interval structure != sorted gaps.** For {0,2,7,9} (C,D,G,A), sorted gaps are (2,5,2), but the quintal interval structure is (7,7,7) from the ordering 0->7->2->9 (C->G->D->A). Implementation must search all 24 permutations of the 4 pitch classes to find one where all 3 forward intervals (mod 12) are in {6,7,8}. Use a const array of all 24 permutations of [0,1,2,3].

**T/I group (Z12 x| Z2):** The semidirect product of transposition and inversion. Order 24. Partitions the 228 legal chords into 14 orbits.
